Live Time: Redesigning a Mobile App
to Drive Growth

Internship

  • Company Live Time
  • Role Product Designer
  • Timeline 9 Weeks
  • Team Founder Product Manager UX Designer
  • Status Shipped
  • Tools Figma

I led a startup's mobile app redesign as it transitioned to the new Live Time brand. The goal was to modernize the app's appearance while preserving the playful spirit of the original design. I developed the brand identity, built a comprehensive design system, and applied it across the app, which is now live on the App Store.

Challenge

The existing Lynkr brand required modernization to better appeal to its Gen Z audience and drive user growth. Although the founder had already chosen the name Live Time, I was responsible for building a cohesive visual identity and user experience around it.

Before:

Modernizing the Visual Identity for a More Inclusive User Experience

Color Palette

Pink was Lynkr's primary brand color, used throughout the original app. However, user feedback revealed that the original shade made the app feel overly feminine, limiting its broader appeal.

Initially, I considered replacing Live Time’s primary color, pink, but realized it gave the brand a unique identity and helped it stand out from the competition. To address user concerns, I refined the pink into a more modern and inclusive shade, pairing it with black and white to establish Live Time’s primary color palette. The new shade preserves the product’s identity without limiting its broader appeal. I also tested the palette with accessibility checkers to ensure sufficient contrast and readability.

To add tonal variety, I introduced a lighter shade of pink and two complementary grays. After finalizing the primary palette, I developed five secondary colors and their lighter variations to provide greater flexibility across the app's design.

Typography

The rebrand introduced two new fonts. Previously, all text used the same sans serif font, causing titles to blend in with body copy and making hierarchy hard to establish. To fix this, I introduced Rockhill Sans as a distinct display font. Its bolder, slightly stylized shape creates clear contrast from body text while keeping the brand feeling sleek and playful. This font is used for branding and display text throughout the app.

For readability, I chose Hanken Grotesk as the primary font for all key information because of its readability, ensuring the app remains clear and easy to navigate. Compared to other sans serif fonts, I found Hanken Grotesk to feel more inviting.

Brand Logo

The flamingo logo was retained but redesigned to align with the new modern look. The redesign, first sketched on paper and then refined in Adobe Illustrator, uses clean, simple lines to make the logo easier to recognize and even draw from memory. While the initial redesign retained the sunglasses, I found that they made the logo harder to read at smaller sizes. The redesign is highly versatile, allowing the logo to be seamlessly incorporated into a text-based version.

Redesigning 150+ Screens into a Cleaner, More Intuitive App Experience

With the new visual identity defined, I redesigned every screen in the app to bring the rebrand to life. The goal was to simplify the experience and deliver a clean, intuitive interface inspired by modern apps like Airbnb.

I began by greyboxing a low-fidelity version of the app in Figma to refine user flows and layouts. As I designed, I limited the amount of information users needed to process on each screen to the essentials, making it easier for them to create and find their own events.

I reviewed the low-fidelity designs with the team to finalize the flows and layouts. Our initial goal was to launch by the end of summer, so we decided to move forward and gather user feedback rather than delay the release for additional testing.

Key Features

Home Page

A personalized feed surfacing upcoming events and activity from the user’s community. I drew inspiration from Airbnb’s homepage layout because it offers a proven approach for browsing a large and varied set of options. For the event cards, I studied competitors such as Partiful and Luma and found that the event name, date/time, location, and host required the greatest visual weight, with the attendee count serving as social proof of a strong turnout.

Event Page

When designing the event page, I gave the event name and RSVP count the greatest visual weight, followed closely by the time and location, so users could quickly scan the details and decide whether they were interested in attending. Previously, all information had equal visual weight, making the page difficult to scan.

Group Page

A hub for the communities and groups a user belongs to, making it easy to stay connected. I designed the layout to resemble the event page, reducing the effort required to learn a new layout.

Create Event Page

A streamlined flow for hosts to create and publish new events. I designed the flow around the essential information (name, location, and date), placing optional details in settings so users can complete it more efficiently.

Launching the Rebranded App

The rebranded app was released on the App Store as Live Time in December 2025.

Reflection

Key Takeaway

I was entrusted early in my career with leading a high-impact startup rebrand. Grounding every decision in user-centered design, I delivered a rebrand I’m proud of while sharpening my skills in branding, UI/UX design, and design systems.

What would I do differently today?

I would have implemented analytics following the rebrand's release to track user flows, identify drop-off points, and prioritize future functionality improvements based on user behavior.

I would have experimented with connecting Claude Code to the design system to accelerate design exploration and prototyping.
