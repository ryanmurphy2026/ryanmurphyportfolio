Love Island USA: Redesigning the Official App to Match the Show's Energy

Personal Project

  • Role Product Designer
  • Timeline 1 week
  • Tools Figma Claude Code

Love Island USA is a hugely popular reality show on Peacock where contestants spend a month on an island, isolated from the outside world, in search of love. It's an entertaining show to watch with friends, with viewers playing an active role by voting through the Love Island USA app to decide which islanders are eliminated.

The official app looks and functions poorly. It currently has a 1.9-star rating on the App Store, with reviews consistently echoing the same complaints.

Challenge

This is clearly a missed opportunity for Peacock. Given the app's high level of engagement, it could serve as an effective platform for advertising, merchandise sales, and deeper audience interaction. I challenged myself to redesign the official Love Island USA app, using the project as an opportunity to explore how AI tools could fit into my design workflow.

Uncovering Why Fans Dislike the App

To better understand why the app is so disliked, I spoke with three friends who are fans of the show. Their responses were telling:

"It sucks and the UI is clunky."

"It looks archaic."

"I'm not inclined to do anything besides vote."

These conversations revealed two key pain points: the app looks outdated, and its layout makes it difficult to find information. My own audit of the app surfaced the same issues:

  • The bottom navigation bar includes two separate buttons for purchasing items, creating unnecessary redundancy.
  • Information about the Islanders is difficult to find at first glance.
  • The color palette doesn't reflect the vibrant, recognizable aesthetic of the Love Island brand.

Designing for Two Types of Viewers

To help guide my redesign, I created two personas representing the primary types of Love Island USA viewers:

The Superfan

  • Watches every episode
  • Engages in online discussions
  • Votes consistently

The Casual Fan

  • Watches two to three episodes per week
  • Doesn't always remember who's who or what happened recently
  • Votes when prompted

It was also important to consider the stakeholders' needs throughout the design process, balancing user needs with Peacock's business goals of driving revenue through advertising and merchandise sales.

From Wireframes to High-Fidelity Screens, with AI as a Collaborator

I began with wireframes. I experimented with using Claude to generate them, but it lacked context about the underlying design problem and produced layouts that didn't reflect real user needs. It also made iteration difficult, so I designed the low-fidelity wireframes in Figma instead and shared them with my friends to gather feedback on the layout before incorporating their input into the next iteration.

Next, I built out a design system, pulling colors and typography directly from the show's branding. Claude Code was useful for expanding the system quickly, handling simple components like buttons and text fields well, but it struggled with more complex components, such as cards, so I built those myself.

Once the design system was finalized, I moved on to high-fidelity designs. To speed up the process, I used Claude Code to generate an initial pass using my low-fidelity wireframes and design system as references, then refined the output to match my vision.

Key Features

Feature #1: Home Screen

The home screen aligns with modern app experiences.

Feature #2: Islanders Tab

Added an Islanders tab to the navigation bar. This helps casual fans keep track of who's coupled up and who's still in the villa, while giving superfans quick access to their favorite contestants' social media.

Feature #3: Voting

Reimagined the voting flow to reduce friction and confusion.

Feature #4: Shop Page

Added a Shop page to encourage browsing. I chose not to implement a full purchase flow because it didn't fit the scope of the project. Instead, tapping a product redirects users to Peacock's website, which felt like the more practical approach.

Feature #5: Extras Page

Refined the Extras page to improve its visual hierarchy.

Reflection

AI is most effective with clear direction. AI performed best when I provided clear instructions and concrete references. It accelerated execution and produced results much closer to my vision, though I still needed to refine the output before arriving at the final design.
